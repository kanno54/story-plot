They say that at the edge of a certain entertainment district, a man wearing a fox mask can be found standing there.

Holding a sign for a girls’ bar, he slips into the city each night.
He does not call out to just anyone.
It is always the other person who speaks first.

“…Can I do it with you, too?”

Why they say it, even they do not understand.
They simply find the words leaving their mouths before they know it.

That night, the one who spoke was a hot-tempered man.

Broad-shouldered, thick-necked, with deep lines between his brows.
A man marked by countless fights, who seemed to have lived by glaring others down.

The fox-masked man merely nodded quietly.

That was enough.

—What followed took place inside a room no one else knows.

When the fox mask is removed, the face beneath is not that of a human.

The ears are pointed.
The pupils split vertically.
When he smiles, faint fangs show.

And yet, the other man does not run.

Perhaps it would be more accurate to say he cannot.

After the act is over, the man lies on his back.

Rough breathing.
Sweat-slick skin.
A heat like dying embers lingers in his body.

The fox leans over him and lifts his chin with a finger.

“Relax.”

He says it in a low voice, then presses his lips to the man’s.

At that moment, a faint light begins to unravel from the man’s mouth.

Fine as thread, soft as mist.
They say it is a fragment of the soul.

The fox neither sucks it in nor devours it entirely.
He merely shaves off a small portion.

If he takes too much, the man would die.
If he takes none, the fox cannot survive.

So he always measures carefully.

One fierce glint fades from the man’s eyes.
In its place falls a softer shadow.

“Thank you.”

The fox smiles faintly as he says this.

The next morning, the man wakes in an alleyway.

He remembers nothing of the night before.
Only a slight emptiness deep within his chest remains.

Strangely enough, he finds he no longer angers as easily as he once did.

Some might call it growth.
Others, a change of heart.

But the old ones in the district whisper something else:

That a fox has taken just a small piece of his soul.

And tonight as well, the fox stands there.

Holding a sign for a girls’ bar,
waiting for the scent of a rich soul.

Not taking too much.
Not killing.
Just a little.

And so, they say, the fox continues to live quietly in the modern world.
